Dinner Truce

Privacy Policy · Last updated: October 10, 2026

The short version: Dinner Truce has no accounts, no analytics, and no tracking of its own. Your recipes and decisions are stored only on your phone. Nothing you type, save, or photograph is sent anywhere by the app on its own. The exceptions are listed in full below: the ad and AI features you can switch on, the recipe photographs the app loads so it can show you pictures of dishes, and anything you deliberately share or export.

What we collect

Nothing. Dinner Truce has no servers and no accounts, and we receive no data from your use of the app.

What stays on your phone

  • Your recipe library, favourites, decision history, shopping lists, and collections — stored locally in the app's private storage.
  • Android platform backup and device transfer are configured to exclude the app database, shared preferences, and Preferences DataStore, including the bundled catalog state and user preferences. Recipe photos you attach are excluded too — they live in the app's private storage and are not copied to Google's servers or to a new phone. Exports are always a deliberate, confirmed action by you.
  • One-tap export creates a JSON file — you choose where it goes. It carries your catalog and cooking history plus everything you built in the app: collections, the 7-day plan, your curated ingredient vocabulary, your correction queue, this device's activity trail, and your standing kitchen rules (including allergens) and what the picker has learned. Your OpenRouter API key is deliberately not in it — that is a credential, and a file you may email should never carry one. An unfinished pick session is not in it either. Nothing is uploaded anywhere by the export itself; the file goes exactly where you send it.

When data leaves your phone

  • Sharing a recipe or collection uses the Android share sheet: it sends exactly what you selected to the destination you picked.
  • Recipe photographs — most catalog recipes carry a picture hosted by TheMealDB or Wikimedia, and the app downloads that picture when it draws the card, so it can show you what the dish looks like. That request goes to TheMealDB or Wikimedia, not to us, and it necessarily reveals your IP address and that you are browsing that recipe. It does not include your recipe library, your history, or anything you typed. Photos you attach are different: those never leave your phone (see above).
  • Voice recipe entry uses Android's built-in speech recognition. Audio is handled by Android's speech service under its own terms; the app itself works only with the recognized text.
  • Photo recipe entry reads the photo on your device. Text recognition runs on the phone; the image is not uploaded. The first time you use it, Android may download the small text-recognition model from Google — that is a model, not your photo.
  • Optional AI features (off by default) — if you enable AI autofill in Settings, the recipe text you submit is sent to the configured AI service (today: OpenRouter, openrouter.ai) to draft a structured entry, and the result is clearly labeled. The heading at the top of every intake screen says so; when the feature is on you see it named, and when it's off nothing goes anywhere. Turning the switch off stops this. Only the ingredients and instructions text are sent — never your photos, your history, or what the picker has learned.
  • "Ask Mallet" (optional, user-initiated) — if you tap it, the app assembles a plain-text handoff: the recipe you are looking at, an aggregate taste summary (what this kitchen picks — counts and tendencies only, never names, dates, or your dinner history), and the question you typed. It goes through the Android share sheet to the app you pick (for example Muse, by Meta) — exactly the text the preview shows before you tap Share, and nothing else. There is no API, no account and no server: nothing is sent unless you tap Share, and Muse does not need to be installed for the feature to work. A Chatty/Quiet switch in Settings hides the feature entirely.
  • Android platform backup is configured to exclude everything this app stores, so the platform does not copy it to your Google account. This is narrower than "backup is switched off", and it is the accurate description.

Recipe data and allergen checks

Recipes in this catalog come from public sources — the provenance badge on each one names them, and "verified" means the kitchen could check. The allergen flags on each recipe are inferred automatically by a word check on your phone running against the ingredient list. That check is deliberately simple and can miss things, and can over-flag: a word list sees the word "tortilla" and cannot know it is corn, not wheat, and it cannot know what is inside a jarred or composite product. When you set allergens in Settings, recipes that trip your rules are kept off the table before a pick is offered, but that is a filter, not a guarantee — and the auto-check that powers it can still be wrong.

Always check the ingredients yourself if you have a food allergy. Allergen information here is not medical advice. Your allergen rules are stored locally, excluded from Android's platform backup, are not part of the "Ask Mallet" share text, and travel only in an export you deliberately create.

Ads (optional bonus rounds only)

The app shows no banners, no interstitials, and nothing between you and the core picker. The only ads are rewarded ads, and an ad is only ever shown after you tap a button that says so — one of: "watch a short ad" on a bonus round, "One more round · watch a short ad" when the kitchen closes with no alternatives left, or the optional embellished share card. The Decider, the first round, the reset, plain sharing and export are never ad-gated. If an ad can't load (for example offline), the action falls back to the free path.

Rewarded ads are served by Google AdMob, which receives your device's advertising ID, ad-interaction data (which ads were shown, tapped, or watched to completion), an approximate location (city-level, derived by Google from your network — never your precise position), and may use the Android Topics API, which groups the apps you use into on-device interest categories so ads can be less random. It may also show a consent form (in regions that require one) before any ad is requested. Google's own privacy policy governs that data: policies.google.com/technologies/ads.

Everything else — your recipes, history, and the taste preferences the picker learns from your own ratings — stays on your phone and is never part of any ad request.

Permissions

  • Microphone — only while you dictate a recipe.
  • Notifications — only for the optional dinner reminder you set.
  • Internet — to load the recipe photographs described above, for the optional rewarded ads (Google AdMob), and if you enable it, AI autofill.
  • Advertising ID — declared by Google's ad SDK so rewarded ads can be measured and so you can be shown a consent form where one is required. It is only ever used for that; if you decline ads it is not used.

The release build also carries a small number of permissions added by Google libraries rather than by this app (ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE for ad and reminder scheduling, RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED and WAKE_LOCK so a reminder you opted into still fires after a reboot, and the ad-services ID permissions). None of them can read your recipes.

Children

Dinner Truce is not directed at children under 13.
